Orbit — Privacy notice (draft for maintainer review)
Updated: 14 October 2026

This English-language notice describes the desktop app under the GDPR. It is a draft, not legal advice. The maintainer must confirm lawful bases and whether a DPO is required before distributing it.

Controller and privacy contact
The data controller is Kielblock Dev, represented by Pedro Kielblock. For privacy questions and access, correction, deletion, portability, objection or consent withdrawal requests, contact Pedro Kielblock at pedro@kielblock.dev. Do not post credentials, logs or personal information in public issues. A formal controller address and any required DPO designation remain to be confirmed; use the same email for data protection inquiries.

Data, purpose and proposed lawful basis
The app reads Claude's existing OAuth token from macOS Keychain or Claude's credentials file solely to retrieve account quota data over HTTPS from Anthropic. It invokes the installed Codex CLI app server to check the authentication type and request current ChatGPT account quotas. The authentication check can return account details such as an email address; Orbit inspects only the authentication type and does not retain, display or log those details. Codex manages its existing credentials and any token refresh. Orbit no longer reads Codex session logs or conversation content. It invokes the installed Antigravity CLI to retrieve quota information; that CLI manages its own authentication and network requests. Quota data, reset times, update times and errors appear locally. The proposed basis for these necessary operations is performance of the service requested by the user (Article 6(1)(b)); the maintainer must confirm applicability.

Software updates use unauthenticated HTTPS requests to check the public pkielblock/orbit repository on startup and every 24 hours while the app is running. You may also check manually. Clicking Update & restart downloads and installs the release and relaunches Orbit. GitHub receives release metadata and download requests, including connection information such as your IP address. Orbit sends no GitHub credentials. The proposed lawful basis for update checks and requested installation is legitimate interests in maintaining app security and reliability (Article 6(1)(f)); the maintainer must assess and confirm this basis.

Retention and control
The app retains its quota display in memory until it exits. Claude usage responses are cached for five minutes. Rate-limited requests use a minimum five-minute cooldown, increasing to one hour after repeated failures and honoring longer server-specified waits. Last successful quotas may remain visible during temporary failures and are labeled with their original fetch time. It creates no database or account and does not copy credentials. Existing credential files, session logs, Keychain items and CLI caches are controlled by the respective provider tools; their retention follows those tools' settings. The connection screen guides sign-in through provider tools, opens installation documentation in your browser, and copies only a sign-in command to the system clipboard when you request it. Your clipboard manager may retain that command; no credentials are copied. Quit the app to stop access and clear in-memory data. Remove the login item to prevent automatic startup. Delete or correct original records through the respective provider tools. The optional --dump and --snapshot commands export displayed usage locally at your request; you control retention of those exports.

Release metadata and the last update check time remain in memory until exit. Requested updates temporarily store the disk image, staged application and previous application in a private .Orbit-update-* folder beside the installed app. These files are removed after installation or a handled preparation failure. If restoration fails, the previous app is preserved there for recovery; interruptions may also leave temporary files. You can remove those folders after recovering the app. Files inherit the device's storage encryption; enable FileVault to encrypt them at rest. Account and credential access audit events contain status, provider name, timestamps and random event identifiers only; they contain no account identifiers, emails, paths, tokens or quota values. Audit output goes to standard output, with retention controlled by the process that launched Orbit. Installation audit events contain operation status, timestamps and random event identifiers, with no account identifiers, tokens, file paths or usage data. They are written to standard output; any retention is controlled by the process that launched Orbit.

Recipients and transfers
The app sends Claude authentication and quota requests to Anthropic. OpenAI and Google/Antigravity receive account authentication and quota requests made by their installed tools at Orbit’s request, including connection information such as an IP address. Codex credentials stay with Codex; Orbit disables analytics for its short-lived app-server process. Orbit does not upload conversation logs. These independent providers may process data internationally under their own terms and safeguards; see https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy, https://openai.com/policies/privacy-policy/ and https://policies.google.com/privacy. There is no developer-operated server or analytics processor. Provider roles and applicable transfer safeguards must be confirmed by the maintainer.

GitHub receives update requests and may process them internationally under its own terms and safeguards; see https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement. Orbit does not upload provider logs or quota data to GitHub.

Cookies and analytics
The app has no advertising, analytics, tracking SDKs or browser cookie features. No non-essential tracking is enabled. External links open in your browser and follow the destination's policies.

Rights
You may request access, correction, deletion, portability, restriction and objection where applicable, withdraw any consent, and complain to your local supervisory authority. The maintainer cannot delete provider-held records; exercise those rights with the provider as well. Local-only records can be controlled through your filesystem and provider tools as described above.
